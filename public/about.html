About Bharat Bhojan

A curated educational prototype exploring relational database design and web development applied to traditional Indian food heritage.

🍲 What is Bharat Bhojan?

Bharat Bhojan (Traditional Indian Food Knowledge Information System) is a database-driven academic web application built for BCA 5th-Semester Project #25. It provides an organized, searchable digital repository documenting traditional Indian regional foods, indigenous ingredients, seasonal (Ritu) adaptations, traditional cooking techniques, and cultural context.

Unlike generic recipe blogs that focus solely on rapid step-by-step measurements, Bharat Bhojan positions food within its broader cultural, historical, and ecological context—examining why particular dishes emerged, how ancient preservation methods (like fermentation, sun-drying, and ash-filtering) functioned, and how seasonal cycles governed traditional culinary habits.

📜 Indian Knowledge Systems (IKS) Foundation

In this project, Traditional Indian Food Knowledge refers to the accumulated, time-tested culinary wisdom developed across Indian communities over centuries. Key aspects documented include:

  • Ritu-Charya (Seasonal Discipline): Aligning food consumption with the six traditional Indian seasons (Shad-Ritu) to maintain equilibrium with local climate conditions.
  • Indigenous Preservation & Chemistry: Ancient natural preservation wisdom such as natural wild fermentation (Dosa, Dhokla, Misal), alkaline filtration (Assamese Kolakhar), sun-cured rinds (Goan Kokum), and earthen baking (Bihari Litti, Odia Chhena Poda).
  • Agrarian & Ecological Alignment: Use of drought-tolerant native grains (sattu, maize, millets) and wild greens (bathua, sarson) adapted to specific soil and weather zones.
  • Cultural & Festival Context: Ritual significance in sacred prasad (Chhath Puja Thekua, Jagannath Puri Dalma, Onam Sadya Avial).

💻 Importance of Digital Documentation

Traditional culinary knowledge is often distributed across rare monographs, regional publications, and familial oral traditions. As dietary patterns modernize, indigenous ingredients and seasonal cooking traditions risk being forgotten.

Computer science offers practical tools—relational databases, structured normalization, and search APIs—to catalog, cross-reference, and make this knowledge systematically discoverable. Bharat Bhojan demonstrates how fundamental database principles directly support cultural preservation.

🎯 Project Objectives

  • Digitally document selected traditional Indian dishes with verified cultural and culinary attributes.
  • Design and implement a normalized relational database (3NF) managing foods, ingredients, seasons, and references.
  • Build an intuitive, responsive web portal supporting multi-criteria filtering, keyword search, and detail views.
  • Develop a secure, session-authenticated administrative portal for full CRUD operations.
  • Demonstrate software engineering fundamentals without depending on heavy commercial frameworks.
  • Promote informed awareness of India’s multifaceted regional food heritage.

⚙️ Technology Stack & Architecture

Frontend Layer

HTML5, CSS3, Vanilla JavaScript (No React/Tailwind)

Backend Layer

Node.js, Express.js (RESTful MVC Architecture)

Database Management

SQLite (node:sqlite) + SQL Query Engine

Security & Authentication

bcryptjs Password Hashing, express-session

⚖️ Academic Scope & Limitations

Academic Positioning: This system is a curated educational prototype developed for academic demonstration. It does not claim to represent an exhaustive encyclopedia of all sub-regional Indian dishes or assert an absolute "single authentic recipe" for any food.
  • No Medical or Health Claims: The platform documents cultural traditions and historical dietary habits. It does not provide medical, therapeutic, or nutritional health advice.
  • Regional & Household Variations: Ingredients, ratios, and cooking styles naturally differ across villages, communities, and households. Baseline entries reflect common verified patterns.
  • Curated Seed Size: Contains 37 carefully researched regional dishes across 25 states and 6 geographical regions chosen to demonstrate geographic diversity and relational database design.
